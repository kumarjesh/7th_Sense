1. Executive Summary & The Offline Imperative

For a visually impaired person, walking autonomy cannot depend on an internet connection. Pedestrians routinely enter underground subway stations, basement elevators, steel-reinforced transit tunnels, building corridors, and rural roads where cellular data drops completely.

If an assistive vision aid relies on sending camera frames to a cloud server to calculate obstacle distances, the user is rendered electronically blind and unsafe the instant signal is lost.

The TriNetra Architectural Guarantee

TriNetra uses a Dual-Layer Perception Architecture. All safety-critical spatial perception (real-time computer vision, 3D binaural audio radar, looming hazard detection, acoustic target beacon, and tactile gestures) operates 100% on-device on the smartphone’s local GPU and audio hardware. Zero cloud requests are required for pedestrian mobility.

2. Rule 0: First-Time Warmup & Service Worker Pre-Caching

Before taking the phone into offline mode, perform a one-time setup while connected to Wi-Fi or cellular data:

  1. Open the App Once with Internet: Visit the TriNetra PWA URL in Google Chrome (Android) or Safari (iOS).
  2. Automatic Model Download: The PWA Service Worker (powered by Workbox) downloads and permanently precaches all HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and the lightweight 2.5 MB TensorFlow.js MobileNetV2 model weights into the browser's persistent IndexedDB and Cache Storage.
  3. Install as PWA: Tap the browser menu and select "Add to Home Screen" or "Install App". This pins TriNetra as a standalone full-screen native mobile application.

3. Live Airplane Mode Demonstration (Step-by-Step)

Follow this exact sequence in front of judges, evaluators, or test audiences to conclusively prove offline autonomy:

  1. Step 1: Enter 100% Airplane Mode in Full View of Evaluators:
    Swipe down the phone's notification or control center. Turn on Airplane Mode (✈️). Explicitly turn off both Wi-Fi and Mobile Data. Hold up the phone to show zero cellular signal and the active airplane icon.
  2. Step 2: Relaunch or Refresh TriNetra:
    Close the browser or tap the home screen TriNetra icon. The application loads instantly with zero network errors (no offline "dinosaur" screen).
  3. Step 3: Tap to Initialize Navigation:
    Tap anywhere on the display to unlock the Web Audio API and WebRTC camera. Hear the phone's on-board text-to-speech engine announce:
    🗣️ "TriNetra active. Scanning started."
  4. Step 4: Demonstrate Live Real-Time Spatial Sonar:
    Hold an obstacle (a chair, backpack, or evaluator's hand) 1 to 2 meters ahead of the phone. Point out the sub-100ms response: the 3D acoustic radar chimes dynamically in stereo headphones, scaling frequency from 440 Hz to 880 Hz as the obstacle draws nearer.
  5. Step 5: Demonstrate Target Finder (Seek Object):
    Perform a Two-Finger Swipe Right. The app announces: "Target set to chair. Point camera to locate." Slowly sweep the phone camera across the room. When pointing directly at a chair, hear the high-pitch 988 Hz acoustic beacon tone chime rapidly in both ears.
  6. Step 6: Demonstrate Light Tone Sonification:
    Perform a Two-Finger Swipe Down. Aim the camera toward a window or room light. The audio pitch rises continuously as illumination intensity increases.
  7. Step 7: Demonstrate Hardware Controls & Telemetry:
    Swipe Up to toggle the camera LED flashlight. Swipe Down to hear status: "Full audio mode. Running at 4 frames per second. Path clear."

5. Architectural Isolation Test (Cloud Fallback)

An essential mark of robust engineering is graceful degradation. While in Airplane Mode, perform a Two-Finger Tap (Gemini Scene Description) or Two-Finger Double Tap (Banknote Reader).

Observed Behavior & Evaluator Explanation

The app attempts the cloud call, immediately catches the fetch error, and speaks: "Scene description failed. Please check internet connection."
Key Point for Judges: The obstacle avoidance radar, spatial audio pings, and safety alerts continue executing in the background without freezing, crashing, or stuttering.
